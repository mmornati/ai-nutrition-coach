## Leads

- **The "family plan" white space is real but filling fast.** Yomi (€9.99 whole-family) and a wave of tiny kids-first AI trackers (Sito, KidMeal AI, FamilyPlate AI) are attacking from the child side while incumbents' TOS exclude <13-18. Worth chasing: Yomi's and Sito's actual download/engagement numbers (Sensor Tower/AppFigures) to see if anyone has escaped hobby scale.
- **Reach3 and Antenna Research** are the primary vendors behind the two most-cited 2026 numbers (71% US adults using health apps; 52% of households cancelling a subscription in 90 days). Their raw studies would replace two aggregator chains with primaries.
- **EuropeActive / Deloitte European Health & Fitness Market Report 2026** (published 2026-04) and **Adapty's State of in-app subscriptions 2026** (paywall/pricing × 16k apps) are the two authoritative-but-unread documents to pull next for an EU revenue figure and conversion norms.
- **Contradiction to resolve:** four credible-ish aggregators put global diet/nutrition-app revenue anywhere from $2.9B to $16.9B for 2026, and Europe fitness apps at $3.4B vs $4.2B. A decision (go/no-go) should pair one methodology (e.g., AppFigures/Sensor Tower consumer spend) rather than average the spread.
- **Surprising cross-signal:** the category's most-explicit "privacy-first, no dark patterns, data ownership" story already exists at scale in the self-hosted community (NutriTrace, Waistline, OpenNutriTracker) — none of them attempt OpenPlate's "coach, not calculator" family teaching angle. Positioning whitespace is educational coaching + family, not privacy per se.
- **AI-logging retention data is a single-vendor (Nutrola) assertion** — the field study behind "photo logging ≈ 2x retention" is Nutrola's own. Chase the underlying academic refs (Turner-McGrievy 2013; Jospe 2021; Burke 2011) before building strategy on it.
