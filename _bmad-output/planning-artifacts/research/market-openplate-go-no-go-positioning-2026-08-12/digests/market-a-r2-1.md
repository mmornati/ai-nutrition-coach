## The AI-retention question — verdict and evidence summary

**Verdict: NO — the "≈2x retention" claim is not independently supported; it stays single-vendor (Nutrola).**

What is independently and strongly evidenced this run:
- AI image logging is **fast** — ~2–25 s/dish vs ~12–22 s for non-AI entry, P<.001 (2025 JMIR RCT) — which mechanically supports a "sub-30-second logging" thesis, but the cited study measured task time and accuracy, not retention.
- AI-photo apps are **perceived as far more usable** — SUS 77 vs 53, 75% preference (2022 JMIR RCT) — and photo-based methods reduce underreporting vs diaries/recall (2016 review).

What independently cuts against the plain prior:
- RCTs that directly measured adherence found photo logging did NOT beat manual: identical adherence (60.6% both) in T2DM adults; photographic food records collapsed to 11% ≥once-daily adherence over 6 months in mothers (vs 74% for survey prompts); an image-recognition app showed no engagement lift in a pediatric obesity pilot.
- 83% of users forget to photograph meals — photo-logging adherence is a known fragility that prompts partially repair (JMIR 2024).

Framing: the vendor's "2x" multiplier is **UNVERIFIED**; the plausible *mechanism* (much-lower-logging-effort) is independently supported, but no independent study quantifies a retention multiple either way — i.e., the academic record neither confirms nor refutes the vendor number, and the two most directly on-point adherence studies actually point lower. OpenPlate should treat the 2x figure as a working hypothesis to validate in-product, not as a market fact.
