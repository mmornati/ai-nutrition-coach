## Leads

- **Where the real evidence lives for OpenPlate's positioning:** the load-bearing support for "coach, not calculator" and "family-safety/trust" comes from peer-reviewed and regulatory sources (JMIR abandonment drivers incl. missing knowledge/expert advice; UCL shame/rigidity study; NEDA + Kurbo/COPPA enforcement; EAT longitudinal disordered-eating data; EatWellUK RCT), NOT from vendor claims. That is unusually strong material for a go/no-go — the pain documentation exists independently of the startup wave claiming it.
- **MFP churn moment:** the May-2026 paywall reshuffle + class action + "lost my 10-year history" reviews create migration intent among long-time users (reviewers literally say "now I'm shopping for another app"). Worth chasing who wins those switch searches this quarter — that churn is the addressable market window.
- **Biggest competitor is "do nothing":** ~70% abandonment within 100 days means most people who try tracking quit entirely; winning means beating friction + shame, not beating MyFitnessPal head-on.
- **Coaching/accuracy arms race is at the top of the market** (MacroFactor adaptive-TDEE, PlateLens photo-AI, Cronometer micronutrient depth, eNutri personalized-advice RCT), leaving the bottom of the market (family education, trust, no-dark-patterns, open-source) comparatively contested by claims but thin on independent evidence and on mature products.
- **COPPA is both the moat and the trap:** the Kurbo settlement and 2025 rule updates make kid-adjacent data collection high-risk (facial/biometric data now covered; age gates that "encourage lying" were cited). OpenPlate's under-13 stance and data-local/self-host architecture could be a differentiator — but needs legal validation; self-hosting does not obviously immunize a mixed-audience app.
- **Source conflicts to resolve next round:** (a) MFP "best/largest database" praise (Unstar) vs peer-reviewed "low validity" (Fuel Nutrition/PMC12550805) — biggest ≠ accurate, but coverage is still the reason users stay; (b) PlateLens's ±1.1–1.3% MAPE and "fastest-growing" claims are vendor/aggregator-sourced — neither DAI/Foodvision papers nor independent download numbers were read; (c) no independent verification of MFP's 4.6→4.2 rating drop beyond one trade outlet citing Sensor Tower.

## Gaps

- **No market-size/TAM data** for family nutrition tracking, kids'-nutrition apps, or "coach-style" nutrition apps; no install/download/uninstall numbers for the family entrants (FamilyPlate, Calorique, Famēlia, Swoodie, TinyFuel) to gauge real traction.
- **No independent evidence** on open-source tracker adoption beyond GitHub stars; no measure of whether "self-hosted/self-hostable" resonates with non-technical parents (no consumer survey found on willingness to self-host or pay for privacy/self-hosting).
- **No direct verification** of PlateLens's claimed benchmark results (DAI-VAL-2026-01, Foodvision Bench) — only vendor and review-site restatements; the underlying methodology papers were not read.
- **No effectiveness evidence** (outcome studies, retention data) for the AI coach products (Hobbes, equ, NutriTracker, Noom coaching, PlateLens coach) — only positioning pages.
- **No data on the "do nothing"/paper/manual-tracker alternative** beyond anecdotal reviewer lines (returning to reading packages, uninstalling) and abandonment statistics; no pricing/reach data on paper food diaries or books.
- **No direct evidence on parents' stated willingness-to-pay** for family nutrition tools, or on how families currently share one tracking product (the FamilyPlate claim that per-person logging is "not realistic" for parents is vendor framing, uncorroborated).
- **Kids-migration data:** whether kids/teens who migrate away from tracked dieting go to alternatives or "do nothing" (back to healthful habits without tracking) is unquantified.
