Executive summary

GO — sized small but size it as a mission/community product, not a venture-scale market bet. The evidence clears OpenPlate's go/no-go gate on fit and differentiation; it does not clear it on segment economics, which remain unproven.

Three findings drive the answer:

  1. The claimed position is real — and the slogan is taken. "Coach, not calculator" is saturated at the adult end (≥12 players, including MyFitnessPal's "Nutrition Coach" and Cronometer's "Crono Coach") [27]. The genuinely unclaimed cell is family + trust + data-local/self-host — every family entrant is cloud/closed-source, and every self-hosted OSS tracker lacks the coach/family angle [13][28]. The privacy-first story alone is already the norm in the OSS niche; privacy is a differentiator only in combination with family coaching [11][13].
  2. The pain is real and independently evidenced — not just marketed. Peer-reviewed and regulatory sources (JMIR scoping review: median 70% abandonment within 100 days, n=525,824; UCL/Loughborough: ~1/4 of user complaints are shame/demotivation from rigid calorie targets [6]; EAT cohort and a 38-study systematic review linking food-app use to disordered-eating symptomology [8]; FTC's $1.5M Kurbo settlement with algorithmic disgorgement [38]) independently validate the "coach, not calculator" direction and the no-dark-patterns stance [5][9].
  3. A live migration window exists. MyFitnessPal's 2026-05-01 paywall (photo upload, recipe import, per-meal macros moved to Premium; a 48-hr 1★ wave followed, magnitude unverified) [30], two N.D. Cal. class actions (Roberts' deceptive-"free" suit, Shah's cookie suit that survived dismissal) [41], a recurring "I lost my history" complaint pattern [42], and a replacement-blog cluster have opened a timed switcher stream [43].

Biggest caveat: the family/kids sub-segment is economically invisible — no TAM exists, the only verifiable kid-adjacent revenue is ~$49 MRR (KidMeal AI, hobby scale), and general health-app willingness-to-pay base rates are low (~9%) [14][31]. The category's dominant competitor is not another app — it's "do nothing"/quitting: ~65% of users abandon their first calorie app within 90 days (vendor-reported, directional), retention falls to ~8% by Day 30 and ~4% by Day 90 [3][4]. OpenPlate wins or loses on retention through reduced friction and trust, by the evidence in this report — not on capturing a sized market.

Positioning verdict in one line: don't lead with "coach, not calculator" (taken); lead with the unclaimed cell — "the family nutrition coach that is yours to run," aimed at the adult who cooks (82% of households have one default "dining manager," mom-led) [15].

1Market size & direction — direction real, magnitude unresolved

All four 2026 sizing reports agree that diet & nutrition apps grow at double-digit rates (~12–20% CAGR), but absolute 2026 value spans a 6x spread — $2.9B to $16.9B [1] Low–med. Europe fitness-app figures conflict similarly ($3.0B @ 13.4% vs $3.3B @ 26.5%) [1]. Direction and double-digit persistence to ~2030–36 are high-confidence High; any absolute number chosen for a pitch must name its methodology, and the spread should be disclosed rather than averaged [2] Medium.

2Reachable segment & behavior — the retention cliff is the load-bearing constraint

  • Adoption is broad but shallow: 71% of US adults use health apps / 64% use devices (Reach3, n=1,043); 63.4% used an mHealth app in the past year (NIH NCCIH) — but historical daily-use (~19%, Gallup 2019) and retention norms (Day-30 ~8%, good ≈12%, Day-90 ~4%) show an install-vs-active chasm [3][4] Low–med.
  • Food-logging specifically: ~65% abandon the first calorie app within 90 days; median lifetime usage 11–14 days [4] Low–med (vendor-reported single chain; treat as directional). The 2026 quit voices: loss of motivation (38%) and free alternatives (25%); subscription-fatigue cancellations at record levels (47% of consumers cancelled ≥1 subscription in 2026; 52% of US households cancelled one in the past 90 days) [4].
  • The parent decision-maker is knowable: fresh 2026 data (n=2,000 US parents with school-aged kids) — 82% of households have one default "dining manager" (mom), moms run 71% of weekly meal decisions vs dads 58%, and the meal-management emotional load is stress 30% / overwhelm 25% / burnout 21% (moms 23% vs dads 14%) [15] Medium. Parents already believe eating habits are lifelong but mostly feel unequipped — 34% confident shaping them; 48% can't tell which foods are healthy (historical, but stable context) [16] Medium (historical).
  • Absence confirmed: no ≤2-yr study exists on how parents choose nutrition/food-tracking apps; family food apps score well on functionality and poorly on engagement (JMIR screening of 2,881 apps) [15].

3Pains & quit-drivers — friction, shame, and missing knowledge

  • Friction is the dominant, physical quit driver: manual logging runs 5–10 min per meal, 20–30 min/day (~3h/week) [17] Medium. The documented failure sequence: unlogged weekends, missing restaurant entries, "close-enough" database picks drifting 200–500 kcal/day, snack-heavy days, the week-6 motivation cliff, travel resets [18] Medium.
  • Shame is the emotional quit driver: UCL/Loughborough (~60k posts on 5 apps): ~1/4 of negative posts are shame/disappointment/demotivation; apps set targets from weight goals, not public-health benchmarks (including −700 kcal/day); streak/notification guilt causes avoidance [6][9] High.
  • Missing knowledge/content is an abandonment driver (JMIR scoping review driver #4, incl. lack of professional advice) — dieters dislike high-input, numbers-first apps and want motivational support [7] Medium — but there is causal evidence for coaching: empirical EatWellUK RCT (n=187) showed web-delivered automated personalized nutrition advice improved diet quality vs population guidelines [5][35][36].
  • AI-photo logging is proven fast, not proven sticky: independently verified — photo logging is ~2–25s/dish vs ~12–22s manual, 86% vs 68% accuracy (n=42); usability SUS 77 vs 52.9 [19] High. But the "AI ≈ 2x retention" claim is single-vendor (Nutrola) and NOT independently supported [20] Unverified multiplier; directly on-point adherence studies are negative or neutral (identical photo-vs-paper adherence in T2DM; mothers' photo-record adherence collapsing to 11% vs 74% over 6 months) [19]. Treat the retention-multiplier as a working hypothesis to instrument in-product.

4Competitive landscape — the only occupied slot is the slogan itself

  • "Do nothing"/quitting is the biggest competitor (~65% abandon the first calorie app within 90 days, ~70% stop within 100 days) [4][5]. Professional coaching substitutes are priced far above apps — dietitian consults $100–250 (follow-ups $75–175), online coaching $99–200/mo [39] Medium — stretching the value gap OpenPlate's free coach fills.
  • The adult "coach" arrow is fully occupied, including by incumbents — MFP (in-app "Nutrition Coach"), Cronometer ("Crono Coach"), plus Goodbite, Alma, Hobbes.health, NutriTracker.io, Noom, equ, MyPlateCoach, Carbon, MacroFactor [27] Medium.
  • The family + trust + data-local/self-host cell is unclaimed. Every family entrant found is closed-source/cloud/subscription (FamilyPlate AI, Calorique Family Kitchen, Famēlia, Swoodie, TinyFuel, MealSync, Famnom, Yomi) [13], and the kids-first entrants are closed freemium too (Ummy, Sito, KidMeal AI) [24][46]. Every self-hosted OSS tracker (Food You, OpenNutriTracker, Waistline, NutriTrace, Dietry, PANTS, Nutritheous, NutriNutri [23]; SparkyFitness is the closest family+privacy analog [12]) lacks the coaching/educational layer [45] Medium (absence of evidence). Nobody retrieves the combination.
  • The kids-first wedge is live and already speaks OpenPlate's language ("no streaks, no guilt", "nutrient floors, not calorie ceilings") — but at hobby scale with near-zero traction: Ummy has 0 ratings (~8 downloads); Sito has 8–10 ratings [24][46] Medium.
  • BYOK/self-host is validated as a real pattern by Fud AI (13 LLM providers incl. local Ollama, keys encrypted in Keychain, "free, open source, privacy first") — yet even Fud AI added paid "Premium Gemini" tiers ($6.99/wk, $199.99/yr), and its site copy ("fully free, no subscription") directly contradicts its App Store IAPs [22] High. That contradiction is the framing-honesty trap OpenPlate must avoid.

Load-bearing teardown notes

  • Cal AI's acquisition ARR resolved to $30M+ (TechCrunch primary 2026-03-02; $40M is Dealroom, $50M ARR-Club — secondhand) [44] High — and MFP's CEO (Mike Fisher, in the acquisition interview) concedes that Cal AI serves "fast, don't-make-me-think" users, leaving deep teaching/coaching unclaimed [44].
  • Between Fud AI, Nutritheous, and NutriNutri, the OSS-AI niche is full of BYOK adult calorie tools; none teach, none target parents. No player in the teardown has a mature product in the family-coach cell [45] Medium.
  • MFP's scale — ~$310M revenue 2025, 220M registered, a 2018 breach of >150M accounts — sets the stakes of the migration window [25][40] Medium, while its telemetry breadth (up to eight third-party trackers in 2025 reporting) is the framing contrast to OpenPlate's data-local promise [26] Medium.
  • The category's OSS user voice skews to feature wishlists and "not a medical application" disclaimers [29].
  • And the wedge voice is already in the review stream: "MyFitnessPal is great at counting, but not coaching." [50] Medium.

6Regulatory & family-safety — a moat available, and a trap to be designed around

  • COPPA is now both stronger and more specific (2025 amendments, finalized Federal Register 2025-04-22; full compliance date 2026-04-22, already passed): "personal information" now includes biometric identifiers (facial templates/voiceprints); separate verifiable parental consent is required for third-party disclosures (targeted advertising, AI training); the "support for internal operations" carve-out means local inference = internal, third-party API AI = disclosure [38] High.
  • Kurbo precedent is the cautionary tale: FTC $1.5M settlement, data deletion, and the first algorithmic disgorgement; the complaint alleged the age-screen "signaled" kids could register by stating ≥13 (per regulatory analyses) [38]. FTC's 2026-02-25 policy statement offers an age-verification "verify, don't profile" lane (collecting a child's data solely to verify age, without parental consent) [48] Medium.
  • Design-legal safe lane for OpenPlate: plain plate photos without face/voice recognition are not per se biometric; meal logging on a parent's account with kids as observed profiles avoids the under-13 account matrix; adults-only self-hosted core sidesteps most exposure. But self-hosting ≠ immunity — exposure follows direction/audience, not server location. Any open under-13 onboarding re-enters the full compliance matrix [47] High.
  • Double-edged finding: the same evidence (FTC/COPPA, NEDA, EAT cohort) makes family-safety a genuine trust opening and kids-food-tracking a reputational/compliance minefield — pediatric guidance counsels avoiding calorie language for young children (an explicit under-12 cutoff appears only in RDN-aligned marketing, low; the disordered-eating association itself is well-evidenced) [8][37] Med–high; child profiles in OpenPlate must track "are they getting what they need," not calories.

7GTM & pricing — economics support the low-cost, annual-plan reality

  • Sub-market mechanics favor OpenPlate's shape: Health & Fitness is the only category where annual plans dominate (60.6% of H&F subscription revenue) and has the highest trial-to-paid (35.0%); category median prices $12.99/mo / $38.42/yr; EU prices are 29–39% higher [14] High (data), tiny sample. Kids' apps already price around $50–60/yr [14].
  • Base rates are brutal: 57.7% of new subscription apps never cross $1,000 lifetime; 8-in-10 never reach $10K, and only 7.9% ever cross $100K (Lifestyle worst at 97.9%) [49] High; willingness-to-pay base rate ~9% (HK, n≈3,000; a comparable segment's median WTP ~US$6.5) [31] High (sample).
  • Privacy is a trust/retention asset, not a pricing lever: no monetary "privacy premium" survives scrutiny (the "3x" claim is vendor marketing, excluded [34] N/A (−)); what is proven is that privacy protections raise willingness to share (JAMA conjoint: protections = 51.5% of decision weight) [32] High and that consumers distrust tech companies with health data (7% vs 64% for a doctor; entity-sharing fell 3.4→2.7 per person 2020–2023) [33] Med–high. Trust buys retention and distribution, not price — consistent with OpenPlate's free/$0 economics ("the economics are the ethics").
  • Acquisition reality for an open product: H&F CPI $3–6 (iOS) / $1.5–3 (Android); fully-loaded CAC 2–3x CPI [10] Medium. The free/OSS model flips this: distribution is the MFP switcher stream (a live review-to-review migration window) plus the self-host/community channel, not paid CPI [43].

§Cross-dimension insights

  1. The retention cliff is the market's real constraint — and the wedge answers it. Sizing (dim 1), behavior (dim 2), and pains (dim 3) all converge: the industry's problem isn't demand; it's that ~65% quit within 90 days and ~25% of complaints are shame. OpenPlate's "no-stigma coaching + one-tap logging" is aimed at the two proven quit-drivers simultaneously — friction and shame — which is the strongest structural argument for the product.
  2. Capturing the migration window and dodging the slogan are the same GTM decision. The MFP switcher stream exists because incumbents paywalled photo logging; the co-opted slogan means OpenPlate's copy must point at the family + yours-to-run cell instead [41].
  3. COPPA turns the "kids" decision into an architecture decision. The childhood-tracking evidence (disordered-eating risk, Kurbo) plus the fresh regulatory regime means child profiles are only viable under a parent account, in "are they getting what they need" form — which happens to match both the research and the marketing (Ummy/Sito) already claiming "nutrient floors, not calorie ceilings" [37].
  4. AI belongs in the product, but its retention claim belongs in the roadmap, not the pitch. Speed and usability are independently proven; the 2x-retention multiplier is not. Lead with the track-first/coach-second value and instrument the retention hypothesis after launch [19].
  5. The package — BYOK + self-host + $0 — converts a compliance hazard into an architectural proof. Local/no-keys/no-cloud is the COPPA internal-operations lane made the default; third-party API AI (BYOK paths) carries the separate-consent burden only if OpenPlate markets child-facing profiles [47].

§Contrary evidence

(red team = off in this run; normal validation only. The strongest counter-findings gathered during the loop are reported here.)

  • The AI-photo claim sticks out of the pitch: independent adherence studies of photo logging are negative or neutral — identical adherence to paper (T2DM), 11% photo-record adherence vs 74% survey prompts over 6 months, no adolescent engagement lift [21] High (single sources). A retention strategy that leans on "AI photo = 2x" has no independent support.
  • The kids segment may be a dead end commercially: all traction evidence is hobby-scale; no independent data shows parent willingness-to-pay for family nutrition tools [14] N/A (absence).
  • "Coach" framing adopted by incumbents: MFP and Cronometer now brand themselves as coaches; if coaching is the only claim, OpenPlate is one OSS voice among very well-funded ones. Only the family + self-host combination is defensible [27] Medium.
  • Self-hosting has no demonstrated consumer demand independent of the privacy-curious niche; the mainstream family buyer may not install anything [13] Medium.

§Recommendations

Each is bound to the decision and to the downstream artifact that consumes it.

  1. GO, sized small. Enter with a mission/community lens and a $0 run-rate; do not build a revenue/VC-size thesis on this segment's economics. High on fit / differentiation Low on segment size
  2. Position for the unclaimed cell, not the occupied slogan: "the family nutrition coach that is yours to run." "Coach, not calculator" remains a useful sanity-check internal phrase — never the primary claim. Feeds: brief.md (Introduction/Solution, vision line). High
  3. Primary user = the adult who cooks (the 82%-household dining manager [15]), with kids as parent-managed profiles in "are they getting what they need" mode — never calorie-targets for under-12s. Feeds: brief.md (Who This Serves) → PRD personas. High
  4. Design to the COPPA safe lane: adults-only accounts; no under-13 onboarding; plain-plate photos (no face/voice); local inference by default so AI stays in the "internal operations" carve-out [47]; document the neutral age-screening requirement before any mixed-audience feature. Feeds: architecture constraints; get a legal validation of the under-13 stance. High
  5. Make the migration window the launch channel: MFP switcher content (the "Loyalty Tax" review stream) + self-host community inbound; keep barcode/photo-friction low; publish honest, contradiction-free pricing/AI wording (learn from Fud AI's "free" vs IAP gap [22]). Feeds: GTM plan. Medium–high
  6. Treat "AI photo ≈ 2x retention" as an in-product hypothesis, not a marketing fact; instrument it from day one [20]. One-tap speed is the launch retention lever with real evidence behind it. Feeds: success metrics + product backlog. High on speed Unverified on multiplier
  7. Monetize only the optional AI, honestly (free-model quota → BYOK → local; an opt-in paid AI bundle is defensible if the free core stays whole), since trust raises willingness to share, not to pay [32]. Feeds: brief.md pricing/assumption + PRD. Medium

§Open questions

  1. How do parents choose a nutrition-tracking app? No ≤2-yr study exists; a small primary survey of the dining-manager segment would be the single highest-leverage next artifact.
  2. What is the actual size/economic ceiling of family-nutrition tracking? No TAM, no WTP-for-family evidence; the first-100-users engine is the natural instrument.
  3. Does AI-photo logging improve retention at all? Needs in-product experiment design post-launch.
  4. Does any consumer market exist for self-hosting? Only the privacy-curious niche is evidenced; mainstream demand unproven.
  5. What exactly does Fud AI's Premium bundle (recurring vs one-time) comprise? Answering this confirms the honest AI-monetization shape OpenPlate could copy or improve.
  6. Independent verification of AI photo-estimation accuracy claims — PlateLens-style vendor benchmarks (DAI/Foodvision) have no independent replication; only general portion-error bounds (15–25%, ~5–10% with depth) are solid.
  7. Roberts v. MyFitnessPal is single-outlet (Consumer Tech Wire side report) despite the underlying filing being a fact; it needs a second source before it becomes load-bearing.
  8. "Lost history" surge after the 2026-05-01 paywall is unquantified — only anecdotal review text exists.
  9. EU primaries unrecovered: Antenna cancellation data and EuropeActive/Deloitte 2026 report remain aggregator-reported here.
