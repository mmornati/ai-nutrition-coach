# Teaching children healthy eating: expert guidance, risks, existing apps, and a safe "family mode" (as of Oct 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-21. Legal/privacy (COPPA/GDPR) is out of scope, apart from one fact about Kurbo that bears on product history. Several primary sources (the AAP Pediatrics full text, the Ellyn Satter Institute's main DOR page) returned 403/404 to the fetch tool, so some AAP and Satter claims rest on reputable secondary summaries. These are flagged where they appear.

## Q1. What expert bodies say about calorie counting, weighing food, "good/bad" food labels and food tracking for children and teens

### Takeaway
Across the AAP, the Satter framework, the national public-health bodies (Canada, France, NHS/UK) and UNICEF, the advice for children is the same. Parents should provide structure and family meals and should model eating varied foods. Weight talk, dieting, pressure, restriction and food-as-reward should be avoided. Children decide how much they eat. None of these sources recommends calorie counting, weighing food or tracking food for children. The AAP 2016 report says outright to discourage dieting and weight talk.

### Cited Findings
**AAP 2016 clinical report, "Preventing Obesity and Eating Disorders in Adolescents" (Golden, Schneider, Wood; Pediatrics 138(3):e20161649, Aug 2016)**
- The report identifies dieting, weight talk and weight teasing as predictors of both obesity and eating disorders, and finds frequent family meals protective against both. — [AJMC summary](https://ajmc.com/view/preventing-obesity-and-eating-disorders-in-teenagers); [AAP News 2016 PDF (Stony Brook)](https://renaissance.stonybrookmedicine.edu/sites/default/files/How%20to%20prevent%20obesity%20without%20eating%20disorders%20AAP%20NEws%202016.pdf)
- It advises pediatricians to discourage dieting, meal skipping and diet pills. Families should be encouraged to talk about healthy eating and being active instead of weight, and should avoid weight comments, including parents' comments about their own weight. — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/newsroom/preventing-obesity-and-eating-disorders-in-teenagers); [Contemporary Pediatrics](https://www.contemporarypediatrics.com/view/when-losing-weight-leads-eating-disorders)
- It warns against encouraging body dissatisfaction or using it as a reason to diet. One cited finding: girls without obesity who dieted in 9th grade were about 3 times more likely to be overweight by 12th grade than girls who did not diet. — [AJMC](https://ajmc.com/view/preventing-obesity-and-eating-disorders-in-teenagers)
- It notes that most adolescents who develop eating disorders were not previously overweight, and that some develop them while trying to lose weight. — [AJMC](https://ajmc.com/view/preventing-obesity-and-eating-disorders-in-teenagers); [PCC blog](https://blog.pcc.com/why-pediatricians-and-parents-should-wait-to-talk-weight-with-teens)
- (Primary text: publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/138/3/e20161649. It was blocked to the fetch tool and has not been verified verbatim.)

**AAP 2023 Clinical Practice Guideline on obesity (Pediatrics 2023;151(2):e2022060640, Jan 2023)**
- The guideline's foundation is Intensive Health Behavior and Lifestyle Treatment: family-based, at least 26 face-to-face hours over 3 to 12 months. It is clinician-delivered, not an app. — [AFP editorial, Oct 2023](https://aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2023/1000/editorial-aap-guideline-childhood-obesity.html)
- It calls for annual BMI-percentile screening from age 2 to 18 and for comprehensive mental and behavioral health screening. Editorials advise clinicians to monitor closely for disordered eating during treatment, noting that children over 11 and those with higher BMI have a higher prevalence of disordered eating (measured with SCOFF). — [AFP editorial PDF](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2023/1000/editorial-aap-guideline-childhood-obesity.pdf); [IHS slides](https://www.ihs.gov/california/tasks/sites/default/assets/File/BP/BP23-AAPGuidelines_Faber.pdf)
- It recommends that adolescents aged 12 and over with obesity be offered pharmacotherapy as an adjunct. — [Physician's Weekly](https://www.physiciansweekly.com/aap-issues-guidelines-for-children-with-overweight-obesity/)
- One children's hospital summary says structured weight-management programmes lower disordered-eating risk in children with obesity. I could not verify this against the guideline text. — [Children's Mercy](https://www.childrensmercy.org/health-care-providers/refer-or-manage-a-patient/connect-with-childrens-mercy/newsletter-the-link/link-newsletter---january-2023/special-report-obesity-cpg/)

**Ellyn Satter's Division of Responsibility (sDOR)**
- From toddlerhood through adolescence, the parent decides what, when and where food is offered, and the child decides how much and whether to eat. — [Ellyn Satter DOR handout via Boston Children's/Framingham](https://ar.childrenshospital.org/sites/default/files/2024-03/Framingham%20-%20Ellyn%20Satter%20Division%20of%20Responsibility.pdf); [UNL Eat at Home PDF](https://eatathome.unl.edu/sites/unl.edu.cehs.eat-family-style-home/files/media/file/EllynSatterDivisionofResponsibilityinFeeding.pdf)
- The goal is "eating competence": children learning to trust their own hunger and fullness cues. — [Penn State Thrive](https://thrive.psu.edu/blog/the-division-of-responsibility/)
- Related guidance from a Montana Team Nutrition handout built on Satter: do not force, reward or shame children about food, and eat meals together. — [Montana Team Nutrition handout, 2016](https://fdn.montana.edu/teamnutrition/smartpleasantmealtimes/Pass%20the%20Peaches%20Handouts%20Fall%202016.pdf)
- The Institute's 12-to-18 material describes family meals as the single most important factor in teen welfare. Parents keep the DOR going while the teen moves toward running their own eating. — [Ellyn Satter Institute, 12–18 product page](https://www.ellynsatterinstitute.org/product/feeding-with-love-and-good-sense-12-through-18-years/)
- The Institute applies a parallel DOR to physical activity: the parent provides structure, safety and opportunities, and the child decides how, how much and whether to move. — [Ellyn Satter Institute](https://www.ellynsatterinstitute.org/the-division-of-responsibility-in-activity/)
- Note: the Institute only permits reproduction of its materials in exact wording with its copyright notice. Any in-app content should paraphrase or link to it. — [search summary of ESI terms; official page](https://www.ellynsatterinstitute.org/how-to-feed/the-division-of-responsibility-in-feeding/) (that URL returned 404 on fetch in Oct 2026 and may have moved)

**Canada's Food Guide**
- Eat meals together as a family as often as possible, put screens away, and let children decide how much they eat. Family-style serving lets each person serve themselves according to hunger. — [Canada's Food Guide, parents & children](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-guide/eating-support/life-stages/parents-children.html)
- Involving kids in planning and preparing meals builds habits and confidence, and picky eaters are more willing to try foods they helped make. The guide lists tasks by age: 6–8 crack eggs and toss salads, 8–11 pack lunch and make a veggie platter, teens follow recipes and own a meal night. — [Canada's Food Guide, involving kids](https://food-guide.canada.ca/en/healthy-eating-recommendations/cook-more-often/involve-others-in-planning-and-preparing-meals/involving-kids-in-planning-and-preparing-meals/); [Food guide kids toolkit "Ready, set, cook" (ages 9–11)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-guide/educators-professionals/toolkit/activities-9-11/ready-set-cook.html)
- Grocery trips can include letting kids pick a new vegetable or fruit, and older kids can read labels. — [Canada's Food Guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-guide/explore/healthy-eating-recommendations/cook-more-often/involve-kids-planning-preparing-meals.html)
- Québec's guidance (French-language): using food as a reward or punishment risks undermining a child's relationship with food. — [Québec.ca](https://www.quebec.ca/sante/alimentation/saines-habitudes-alimentaires/alimentation-des-enfants)

**France: PNNS / Santé publique France, mangerbouger.fr**
- Re-offer a new food regularly without forcing it, spaced over time, sometimes up to about ten tries, and stop insisting after that because the child may genuinely dislike it. Parents model by enjoying the food themselves. Serve the same main dish to the whole family, adapting it if needed. Vary preparations and cook together. — [Manger Bouger: développer les goûts de votre enfant](https://www.mangerbouger.fr/manger-mieux/a-tout-age-et-a-chaque-etape-de-la-vie/enfants-et-adolescents-de-4-a-17-ans/comment-developper-les-gouts-de-votre-enfant-et-son-plaisir-a-manger); [Cuisiner avec les enfants](https://www.mangerbouger.fr/manger-mieux/se-faire-plaisir-en-mangeant-equilibre/cuisiner-maison/pourquoi-et-comment-cuisiner-avec-les-enfants-decouvrez-nos-idees-de-recettes)
- Opposition and food refusal between ages 2 and 6–8 is described as normal. — [Manger Bouger](https://www.mangerbouger.fr/manger-mieux/a-tout-age-et-a-chaque-etape-de-la-vie/enfants-et-adolescents-de-4-a-17-ans/entre-2-et-6-8-ans-votre-enfant-s-oppose-c-est-normal)
- Santé publique France publishes a 40-page parents' guide for ages 4–11 ("C'est trop bon"). — [Guide Manger-Bouger 4–11 ans (PDF)](https://www.mangerbouger.fr/content/show/2132/file/DT05-414-24B%20148-5fx210_GUIDE_40P_MANGER_BOUGER_4-11_ANS_bd_v2.pdf)

**NHS / UK**
- NHS trust pages that draw on Healthier Families advise: do not use food as a reward (use stickers, books or outings instead), keep regular meal and snack routines, eat together with screens off, make water the default drink, expect to offer a food more than 10 times, and treat fussy eating as common and usually temporary. — [West Yorkshire Healthier Together (NHS)](https://wyhealthiertogether.nhs.uk/parentscarers/keeping-your-child-safe-and-healthy/healthy-eating); [County Durham & Darlington NHS FT](https://www.cddft.nhs.uk/services/nutrition-dietetics/children/healthy-eating); [NW London Healthier Together (NHS)](https://healthiertogether.westlondon.nhs.uk/parentscarers/keeping-your-child-safe-and-healthy/obesity) (I did not retrieve the Healthier Families site itself.)

**UNICEF**
- A toddler may need to taste a new food more than 10 times before deciding whether they like it. The advice is to offer it repeatedly, prepared in different ways, without forcing. — [UNICEF Parenting (FR)](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/fr/alimentation-et-nutrition/7-astuces-pour-faire-manger-un-enfant-difficile)

### Inferences
- No authoritative source supports calorie counting, food weighing or numeric intake tracking for children. Their advice runs the other way: away from weight talk, dieting and pressure, and toward structure, family meals, exposure and modelling. A child-facing calorie or gram display would contradict all of these sources.
- The sDOR maps onto the app's roles. The parent (the adult user) owns "what, when, where", which is the menu and the household meals. The child's "how much" should never be measured or scored.
- "Good/bad" food labels are not endorsed by any of these bodies. Their framing is variety, enjoyment and modelling. The Kurbo traffic-light backlash (Q3) shows how labelling for children is received.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the AAP 2016 or 2023 full texts (403), so wording is taken from reputable secondary summaries. I found no explicit AAP sentence on "calorie counting apps" for children.
- I did not retrieve WHO-specific child guidance on food tracking or labelling. WHO coverage here is limited to UNICEF parenting material.
- I did not retrieve the NHS Healthier Families site directly. NHS trust pages were used instead.

## Q2. Evidence on eating-disorder risk from diet and tracking apps for minors and adolescents

### Takeaway
The evidence is suggestive but mostly cross-sectional and drawn from college students and adults. I found no adolescent-specific trial showing that calorie apps cause eating disorders. Clinicians and ED charities nonetheless treat calorie counting and calorie-focused tools as a clear risk for young people. Public Health England blocked under-18s from its own weight-loss app after Beat raised concerns.

### Cited Findings
- College students who regularly tracked calories in a smartphone app also reported more disordered eating (Simpson & Mazzeo). The authors note the direction may run the other way: people with symptoms may be more likely to track. — [Psychology in Action summary](https://www.psychologyinaction.org/2018-4-29-calorie-counting-and-fitness-tracking-technology-associations-with-eating-disorder-symptomology/)
- In a University of Louisville study of 105 patients with eating disorders, about 75% used MyFitnessPal, and 73% of those users felt it contributed to their ED. This is self-report from a clinical sample, so it does not establish causation. — [HealthDay](https://healthday.com/health-news/mental-health/phone-apps-dialing-up-eating-disorders-633747.html)
- Duke Psychiatry's blog "The Trouble with Tracking" discusses clinical concerns about food and fitness tracking. — [Duke Psychiatry](https://psychiatry.duke.edu/blog/trouble-tracking)
- A review of 35 studies (over 52,000 participants, mean age about 17) linked compulsive phone use to disordered eating and body dissatisfaction. It did not isolate calorie apps. — [Homeword summary](https://homeword.com/heavy-phone-use-linked-to-disordered-eating-risk-in-teens/) (secondary source; the original paper was not retrieved)
- Beat (UK ED charity, 2022) was particularly concerned about calorie labels on children's menus, saying they would increase mealtime anxiety for young people with EDs and hinder recovery. — [RCPsych/NCCMH, Aug 2022](https://www.rcpsych.ac.uk/improving-care/nccmh/dyfodol/dyfodol-news/detail/2022/08/23/98-of-people-affected-by-eating-disorders-feel-calories-on-menus-will-negatively-impact-them-new-survey-finds); [TFN Scotland](https://tfn.scot/news/scrap-calorie-labeling-plans-urges-charity)
- In a 2022 survey, 98% of people affected by eating disorders said calories on menus would affect them negatively. — [RCPsych NI, Aug 2022](https://www.rcpsych.ac.uk/members/devolved-nations/rcpsych-in-northern-ireland/northern-ireland-news/detail/2022/08/23/98-of-people-affected-by-eating-disorders-feel-calories-on-menus-will-negatively-impact-them-new-survey-finds)
- Beat campaigned on the NHS "Better Health" weight-loss app, arguing that a low-BMI warning without blocking access was insufficient. Public Health England then added measures to keep under-18s and people with low BMI from using the app. — [Beat news](https://beateatingdisorders.org.uk/news/beat-campaigners-celebrate-improvement-in-nhs-app)
- The AAP 2023 guideline commentary flags a higher prevalence of disordered eating in children over 11 and those with higher BMI, and urges close monitoring during treatment. — [AFP editorial, Oct 2023](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2023/1000/editorial-aap-guideline-childhood-obesity.pdf)
- Project EAT (Neumark-Sztainer et al., Arch Pediatr Adolesc Med 2008; 2,516 adolescents followed for 5 years) found that girls with 5 or more family meals a week had lower odds of extreme weight-control behaviours five years later (OR 0.71, 95% CI 0.52–0.97). The association did not hold for boys. — [PubMed 18180407](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18180407); [UMN Experts](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/family-meals-and-disordered-eating-in-adolescents-longitudinal-fi/)
- Cross-sectional Project EAT data: 18.1% of girls with 1–2 family meals a week reported extreme weight-control behaviours, versus 8.8% of girls with 3–4. — [UMN Experts](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/are-family-meal-patterns-associated-with-disordered-eating-behavi/)

### Inferences
- The precautionary consensus from AAP and Beat, combined with PHE's precedent of excluding under-18s, supports a strict rule: no calorie, gram or weight features for any user under 18.
- Family-meal frequency has some protective evidence, mainly for girls. It is a safe, non-numeric metric a parent-facing coach can encourage.

### Gaps
- I found no RCT or longitudinal study of calorie-tracking apps in under-18s. The evidence base is adult and college samples plus clinical opinion.
- I did not retrieve a 2024–2026 NEDA or Beat statement specifically on tracking apps for minors. (NEDA's helpline restructuring since 2023 means its web resources may have changed. Not verified.)

## Q3. Kurbo by WW: what it did and the criticism

### Takeaway
Kurbo (WW, launched August 2019) was a weight-loss programme for children aged 8–17. It sorted foods with a traffic light, offered paid video coaching and published before/after success stories. Dietitians, ED clinicians, NEDA and a petition of more than 78,000 signatures opposed it. In 2022 the FTC fined it for COPPA violations, and it was unpublished from Google Play in August 2022. It is the clearest cautionary precedent for this project.

### Cited Findings
- WW launched "Kurbo by WW" on 13 Aug 2019 as a behaviour-change programme "to help kids and teens ages 8-17 reach a healthier weight". The app was free, with $69/month for weekly 15-minute video sessions with Kurbo-certified coaches. — [WW press release, PR Newswire, Aug 2019](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ww-launches-kurbo-by-ww-to-help-kids-and-teens-reach-a-healthier-weight-through-a-scientifically-proven-program-virtual-coaching-and-a-free-app-300900812.html); [Eating Disorders Review, Jan 2020](https://edr.iaedpfoundation.com/update-new-weight-watchers-anti-obesity-app-for-children-draws-criticisms/)
- Foods were classed red, yellow or green, with red foods to be eaten only occasionally. Critics said it taught exercise as a way to "earn" food. — [VICE, Aug 2019](https://www.vice.com/en/article/weight-watchers-under-fire-for-diet-and-food-tracking-app-for-kids/); [TIME, Aug 2019](https://time.com/5649964/kurbo-by-ww-app/)
- A Montreal dietitian said it would make kids hyper-focus on food, label foods good or bad, and tie health to body weight. Dietitian Keri Glassman called the before/after photos of children (some as young as 8) body shaming. — [Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.com/ww-weight-loss-app-kids-190921660.html); [CJAD](https://cjad800.com/news/parents-nutritionists-slam-new-weight-watchers-healthy-eating-app-for-kids-1.9822277.html); [HuffPost CA](https://www.huffingtonpost.ca/entry/kurbo-weight-watchers-app-kids_ca_5d5595e1e4b056fafd08ac0a)
- The Melrose Center (an ED treatment centre in Minnesota) said it did not recommend the app for kids or teens. NEDA objected that children were not medically cleared before signing up and that there was no medical surveillance. NEDA issued a statement on 16 Aug 2019. — [Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.com/ww-weight-loss-app-kids-190921660.html); [Wikipedia: Kurbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurbo)
- A change.org petition, "Weight Watchers: remove your weight loss app for kids", had more than 78,000 signatures by mid-August 2019. — [change.org](https://www.change.org/wakeupweightwatchers); [EDR](https://edr.iaedpfoundation.com/update-new-weight-watchers-anti-obesity-app-for-children-draws-criticisms/)
- WW's response: behaviour-based weight programmes do not cause EDs, and the method came from Stanford research. — [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/weight-watchers-under-fire-for-diet-and-food-tracking-app-for-kids/)
- Eating Disorders Review noted that the supporting study (Cueto et al.; 1,120 participants, mean age 12) did not report adverse effects such as distress, body dissatisfaction or disordered eating, and said future studies must assess them. — [EDR, Jan 2020](https://edr.iaedpfoundation.com/update-new-weight-watchers-anti-obesity-app-for-children-draws-criticisms/)
- In March 2022 the FTC and DOJ imposed a $1.5M penalty and required deletion of under-13 data and of algorithms built with it. (This is a privacy matter, noted only as product history.) — [FTC case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/1923228-weight-watchersww); [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/ftc-settlement-ww-weight-watchers-kurbo-children-prvacy/)
- The app was unpublished from Google Play on 16 Aug 2022. — [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/kurbo-by-ww-weight-watchers/org.kurbo.app)

### Inferences
- What drew the criticism: a weight-loss goal for children, colour-coded "red" foods (good/bad labelling), exercise framed as earning food, before/after photos of children, and no medical screening. A family mode should avoid every one of these.
- Even a "scientifically proven" framing and a Stanford pedigree did not protect Kurbo's reputation.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Kurbo's 2024–2026 status on iOS or on WW's site, or whether WW formally discontinued it.

## Q4. Child-focused nutrition education apps, programmes and approaches

### Takeaway
The credible child-facing resources contain no numbers. They use games about building a plate, recipes kids can make, tasting and sensory education, and cooking. The evidence for games is positive but weak: mostly small, short studies using self-report.

### Cited Findings
- USDA MyPlate Kids' Place (archived 2019) offered games ("Blast Off" fuelling a rocket with food groups, "Build-A-Meal", "Combo Kitchen", "Dining Decisions"), songs, activity sheets, recipes and a "MyPlate Champion" pledge. "Smash Your Food" showed sugar cubes, salt and oil in foods. — [Webharvest archive of choosemyplate.gov/children, Jan 2019](https://webharvest.gov/congress115th/20190108210340/https://www.choosemyplate.gov/children); [MSU Extension](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/myplate_kids_place); [Healthy Kids Running Series](https://healthykidsrunningseries.org/healthy-families/9-fun-games-for-you-and-your-children-from-choosemyplate-gov/) (the current MyPlate kids lineup was not verified)
- Food Hero (Oregon State University Extension, SNAP-Ed) has a kids section with online games, activities, videos and recipes "tested and approved by kids in Oregon". — [Food Hero Kids](https://foodhero.org/kids)
- Other public examples: the Food Literacy Center's "create your own MyPlate" game, and WSU Extension SNAP-Ed "Just for Kids". — [Food Literacy Center](https://www.foodliteracycenter.org/broccoli-beet-year/fun-food-games-kids-create-their-own-my-plate); [WSU Extension](https://extension.wsu.edu/yakima/health-wellness/snap-ed/just-for-kids/)
- Canada's Food Guide offers a teacher toolkit with cooking activities for ages 9–11. — [Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-guide/educators-professionals/toolkit/activities-9-11/ready-set-cook.html)
- France's Classes du goût / SAPERE method: created by oenologist Jacques Puisais (with psychologist Matty Chiva), first classes in 1974. It consists of about ten sensory lessons for 8–10-year-olds on the five senses and tasting, and aims to reduce food neophobia through playful curiosity. SAPERE International spread it to other countries. A 2012 French national programme trained teachers and delivered 8 sessions. — [SAPERE: Taste education in France](https://www.sapere-association.com/?p=282); [SAPERE leaflet](https://www.sapere-association.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/depliant_SAPERE_LesClassesduGout.pdf); [DAAF Réunion 2012](https://daaf.reunion.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/Presentation_du_projet_Classe_du_gout_2012_La_Reunion_cle88d1a6.pdf); [Institut du goût](https://www.institutdugout.fr/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/Education-au-goût-Pratiques-en-Nutrition.pdf)
- A review of 43 gamification and serious-game studies found that games can raise fruit and vegetable intake and nutrition knowledge, while advergames promoting snacks increased snack intake. — [Deakin repository (embargoed)](https://dro.deakin.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/Can_games_change_children_s_eating_behaviour_A_review_of_gamification_and_serious_games/20644836)
- One serious game was followed by a 29% increase in broccoli and a 17% increase in cauliflower consumption. — [IJOE](https://www.journals.innovareacademics.in/index.php/ijoe/article/view/58363) (figure from a search snippet; the original was not verified)
- Other reviews report mostly self-reported outcomes, mixed results with most games only partly meeting their aims, and short, small studies. — [IJSG review](https://journal.seriousgamessociety.org/index.php/IJSG/article/view/466); [ABCS Health Sciences review](https://nepas.emnuvens.com.br/abcshs/article/view/2323); [Cureus poster (non-peer-reviewed)](https://www.cureus.com/posters/1722-impact-of-serious-games-on-nutritional-knowledge-and-practices-in-children-and-adolescents-a-systematic-review)

### Inferences
- The proven building blocks are build-a-plate or food-group games, kid-tested recipes, sensory "taste detective" activities and cooking tasks matched to age. None needs intake numbers.
- Avoid the "Smash Your Food" pattern (sugar cubes and salt shown per food) in child-facing content. It is educational, but it edges toward good/bad framing. This is an inference, not a cited expert position.

### Gaps
- I did not verify current (2025–2026) commercial "eat the rainbow" kids apps. Names in the brief such as "Smart Bites" and "Yummly kids" were not confirmed as existing products, and I found no reliable source for them.
- I found no outcome evaluation of the Classes du goût focused on neophobia. The sources are mostly promotional or training material.

## Q5. Effective techniques for children

### Takeaway
The best-supported technique is repeated, unpressured exposure to plain vegetables, roughly 8–15 offers. It works alongside parental modelling, cooking and shopping together, regular family meals, sensory education, and never using food as a reward or punishment.

### Cited Findings
- A meta-analysis of 30 studies (n=4,017, ages 2–5) found repeated taste exposure gave the best pooled effects. Intake rose with the number of exposures, was greater for plain vegetables than flavoured, dipped or energy-added ones, and rose most for unfamiliar or disliked vegetables. — [PubMed 29702128](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29702128/)
- A systematic review rated repeated exposure as well supported in early childhood. It estimates about 8–10 exposures at regular intervals, such as weekly. Caregivers often give up early because they read the child's facial expression as genuine dislike. — [PMC5438436](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5438436/)
- Parents give up after 3–4 refusals on average. — [Institut du goût / Pratiques en Nutrition](https://www.institutdugout.fr/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/Education-au-goût-Pratiques-en-Nutrition.pdf)
- More than 10 tastes (UNICEF), up to 15 or more (Alberta Health Services), about ten spaced tries without forcing (Manger Bouger). — [UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/fr/alimentation-et-nutrition/7-astuces-pour-faire-manger-un-enfant-difficile); [AHS PDF](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/info/nutrition/if-nfs-feeding-toddlers-and-young-children-french.pdf); [Manger Bouger](https://www.mangerbouger.fr/manger-mieux/a-tout-age-et-a-chaque-etape-de-la-vie/enfants-et-adolescents-de-4-a-17-ans/comment-developper-les-gouts-de-votre-enfant-et-son-plaisir-a-manger)
- Regular use of food rewards has been associated with emotional eating from about age 5. Health authorities advise never using food to bribe, coax, punish or reward. — [Allianz Care blog (secondary)](https://www.allianzcare.com/fr/qui-sommes-nous/blog/les-conversations-sur-alimentation-avec-les-enfants.html); [AHS](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/info/nutrition/if-nfs-feeding-toddlers-and-young-children-french.pdf); [Québec.ca](https://www.quebec.ca/sante/alimentation/saines-habitudes-alimentaires/alimentation-des-enfants)
- Modelling: a parent visibly enjoying a food helps the child broaden their palate. — [Manger Bouger](https://www.mangerbouger.fr/manger-mieux/a-tout-age-et-a-chaque-etape-de-la-vie/enfants-et-adolescents-de-4-a-17-ans/comment-developper-les-gouts-de-votre-enfant-et-son-plaisir-a-manger)
- Cooking together is "the best way" to familiarise children with unfamiliar foods, especially vegetables (Manger Bouger). It also increases picky eaters' willingness to try foods (Canada). — [Manger Bouger](https://www.mangerbouger.fr/manger-mieux/se-faire-plaisir-en-mangeant-equilibre/cuisiner-maison/pourquoi-et-comment-cuisiner-avec-les-enfants-decouvrez-nos-idees-de-recettes); [Canada's Food Guide](https://food-guide.canada.ca/en/healthy-eating-recommendations/cook-more-often/involve-others-in-planning-and-preparing-meals/involving-kids-in-planning-and-preparing-meals/)
- Family meals are protective against disordered eating, with longitudinal evidence for girls (see Q2), and the AAP 2016 report also lists them as protective. — [PubMed 18180407](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18180407); [AJMC](https://ajmc.com/view/preventing-obesity-and-eating-disorders-in-teenagers)

### Inferences
- The exposure gap (parents stop at 3–4 tries when 8–15 are needed) is a strong, safe use case for an app. A parent-facing "new food journey" can quietly count offers, not amounts eaten, and encourage persistence without pressure.

### Gaps
- The exposure-count evidence is almost entirely in 2–5-year-olds. I found no trials on 8–15-year-olds.

## Q6. What a "family mode" in an adult-run app could look like

### Takeaway
Based on the sources above (this design is an inference, not an external standard): the adult account holder logs household meals. The coach gives parent-facing guidance on the structure the parent controls under the sDOR: variety, vegetable and new-food exposure, family-meal frequency, cooking together. It never shows per-child amounts, calories, grams, weight or scores. Any child-facing surface is playful and number-free, framed around taste, senses and cooking.

### Cited Findings (supporting design constraints)
- The parent owns what, when and where. The child owns how much and whether. — [Satter DOR handout](https://ar.childrenshospital.org/sites/default/files/2024-03/Framingham%20-%20Ellyn%20Satter%20Division%20of%20Responsibility.pdf)
- Talk about healthy eating and activity, not weight. No dieting, no fuelling body dissatisfaction. — [AJMC on AAP 2016](https://ajmc.com/view/preventing-obesity-and-eating-disorders-in-teenagers)
- Family meals: as often as possible, screens off, family-style serving. — [Canada's Food Guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-guide/eating-support/life-stages/parents-children.html)
- Repeated, unpressured exposure with plain vegetables. — [PubMed 29702128](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29702128/)
- No food as reward or punishment. — [Québec.ca](https://www.quebec.ca/sante/alimentation/saines-habitudes-alimentaires/alimentation-des-enfants); [NHS CDDFT](https://www.cddft.nhs.uk/services/nutrition-dietetics/children/healthy-eating)
- Under-18s were excluded from PHE's calorie-focused weight-loss app after Beat's campaign. — [Beat](https://beateatingdisorders.org.uk/news/beat-campaigners-celebrate-improvement-in-nhs-app)
- Kurbo's failure modes: weight-loss goals for kids, red/green food labels, exercise to "earn" food, before/after photos, no medical screening. — [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/weight-watchers-under-fire-for-diet-and-food-tracking-app-for-kids/); [Yahoo](https://news.yahoo.com/ww-weight-loss-app-kids-190921660.html)

### Inferences (proposed design, for the report writer)
**Parent-facing (adult account only):**
- Log household meals at the meal level ("family dinner: lentil curry, rice, salad"), not per-child portions.
- Calm weekly reflections based on household variety: food groups or plant variety across the week's family meals, number of shared family meals, and new or rejected foods that are still "in rotation".
- A "new food journey" tracker that counts offers of a food (for example "offered 4 times; experts suggest 8–15, keep it low-pressure"), never how much the child ate.
- Prompts drawn from the guidance above: cook-together tasks by age (Canada's Food Guide list), let the child pick a new vegetable at the shop, serve family-style, screens off, model enjoyment, no dessert-as-reward.
- Coach guardrails: never comment on a child's weight, body or amount eaten, and never suggest dieting for a child. If a parent raises worry about weight or eating, give neutral, non-alarming signposting to a paediatrician or registered dietitian.

**Child-facing (optional, playful, number-free):**
- Sensory "taste detective" activities in the Classes du goût / SAPERE style (describe crunchy, sour, smell), build-a-plate or food-group games, age-appropriate recipes, and "eat the rainbow" colour collecting, framed as exploration and not compliance.
- No calories, grams, nutrient bars, points-for-foods, red/yellow/green or good/bad labels, weight, BMI, streaks tied to eating, leaderboards, or food-as-reward mechanics.

**What must NOT be done (consolidated):**
1. No calorie, gram or nutrient numbers or targets about any child, or shown to any child.
2. No weight, BMI or body-shape tracking, goals or comments for children, and no before/after imagery.
3. No good/bad, red/green or "junk vs clean" food labelling in child-facing content.
4. No exercise framed as earning or burning food.
5. No food (or dessert) as reward or punishment, and no gamified rewards for eating amounts.
6. No pressure mechanics such as "clean your plate" goals or bite counters. Track offers, not intake.
7. No unsupervised child accounts for diet tracking. Mirror PHE: no under-18 access to calorie features.
8. Keep a clear medical boundary: the app does not treat childhood obesity. AAP-style treatment is clinician-led intensive family therapy (26+ hours), so the app should signpost and not substitute.

### Gaps
- I found no existing product that implements a parent-facing, number-free family mode in the way described. This looks like white space, but the claim is unverified because the competitive scan here was not exhaustive.
- I found no published evaluation of offer-counting (exposure-tracking) features in parent apps.
