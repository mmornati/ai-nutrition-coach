# Coaching and behaviour-change patterns in coach-style nutrition apps

Research date: 2026-10-21. Sources were found by web search; most were read from search snippets and only a few were fetched in full. Where a figure comes from a secondary source, the note says so.

## 1. How apps say whether a food or meal is "good" (and what the evidence says)

### Takeaway
Every major app turns food into one simple signal: a colour (Noom), a points number (WW), a letter (Fooducate, Nutri-Score), or a 0–100 score (Yuka, ZOE, Lifesum's per-food rating). Signals based on public-health science (Nutri-Score, energy density) have the best evidence behind them, but the effect on real purchases is small. Proprietary scores (Yuka, ZOE) have been criticised as opaque, absolute or simplistic. A coach app is best off describing foods (density, fibre, salt, processing level) and not grading them morally.

### Cited Findings
**Noom (colours by calorie density)**
- Noom gives each food a colour mainly from its calorie density (calories per gram or mL). Green is the least dense, yellow the middle and orange the most dense. Examples: spinach and tofu are green, avocado and black beans yellow, nuts, oils, cookies and fries orange. Noom says orange foods are not off-limits and the goal is balance. — [Noom help: How Noom's Food Color System Works (2025)](https://www.noom.com/support/faqs/using-the-app/logging-and-tracking/food-and-water/2025/10/how-nooms-food-color-system-works/)
- A Healthline dietitian review says the system moves the focus away from calories alone, but warns that some nutrient-dense foods (for example nuts and avocado) land in orange or yellow. — [Healthline Noom review](https://healthline.com/nutrition/noom-diet-review)
- The old colours were green/yellow/red. Some low-quality sites still say "red", but Noom's current help page says "orange". — [Noom help](https://www.noom.com/support/faqs/using-the-app/logging-and-tracking/food-and-water/2025/10/how-nooms-food-color-system-works/)

**WW (WeightWatchers Points)**
- Points combine calories, saturated fat, sugar and protein into one number per food. — [CNN, May 2025](https://edition-prod-cf.sitemirror.cnn.com/2025/05/06/business/weight-watchers-bankruptcy)
- WW has expanded its "ZeroPoint" list to 150+ foods and added an AI food scanner and insurance-covered dietitian access. — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/weightwatchers-nears-bankruptcy-filing-per-report/)
- WW International filed for Chapter 11 in May 2025, with roughly US$1.15–1.5 bn of debt (figures vary by source). Reports blame GLP-1 drugs pulling people away from points and calorie counting. Members fell 12%. — [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/health/article/weightwatchers-files-for-bankruptcy/); [ABC7](https://abc7news.com/amp/post/weightwatchers-files-bankruptcy/16342430/)
- Criticism of zero-point foods is mostly opinion. One example: "fruit shouldn't be free because of its calories." — [Jessica Cording Nutrition blog](https://www.jessicacordingnutrition.com/weight-watchers-free-fruit-draws-criticism/)

**Fooducate (letter grades)**
- Fooducate uses 10 grades from A to D (A, A-, B+, … D). It adds points for fibre, calcium and iron, and takes points away for saturated fat, sodium and sugar. Each food category weights the nutrients that matter for it (fibre counts for bread, not for yogurt). Products are ranked within their category. Additives, HFCS, colourings and processing lower the grade. — [Health Advocate blog, 2014](https://blog.healthadvocate.com/2014/03/wellness-app-review-fooducate/); [TIME Healthland 2012](https://healthland.time.com/2012/08/16/5-great-health-apps-you-should-use-now/slide/best-app-for-healthy-eating-fooducate/). The sources are from 2012–2014, so the current formula is not confirmed.

**Yuka (0–100 score)**
- Yuka's score is 60% nutrition (based on Nutri-Score), 30% additives (a risk scale Yuka wrote itself) and 10% organic status. A registered dietitian calls this weighting arbitrary. — [Mama Knows Nutrition (RD review)](https://mamaknowsnutrition.com/yuka-app-reviews)
- Italy's antitrust authority reportedly criticised Yuka's judgements as absolute and simplistic, and its method as unclear and never approved by a health authority. The case closed after Yuka agreed to change the app. — [Il Sole 24 Ore](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/yuka-app-like-nutriscore-penalises-italias-food-industry-AIynzfzB). This is secondary reporting; the original AGCM decision was not checked.
- Reviewers also note that Yuka scores "potential" risks, ignores the individual's diet, and suggests "healthier" swaps without considering convenience. — [Mama Knows Nutrition](https://mamaknowsnutrition.com/yuka-app-reviews)

**Nutri-Score (A–E) and its 2024 update**
- The algorithm was revised in 2022–2023 by the European scientific committee of the 7 participating countries. The goal was closer alignment with national dietary guidelines. — [Santé publique France, 18 Jan 2024](https://www.santepubliquefrance.fr/en/les-actualites/nutri-score-update-changes-2024)
- What changed: products that are still too sweet or salty score lower. Wholegrain, fibre-rich products score higher than refined ones. Poultry now scores above red meat. Oils rich in healthy fats and some fatty fish score better. Sweeteners now count against drinks, so diet sodas move from B to C–E. 30–40% of products change score. — [Santé publique France](https://www.santepubliquefrance.fr/en/les-actualites/nutri-score-update-changes-2024)
- Dates: the new algorithm took effect 1 Jan 2024 in Germany, Belgium, Switzerland and the Netherlands. France expected it in early 2024, and manufacturers have two years to update packaging. — [Santé publique France](https://www.santepubliquefrance.fr/en/les-actualites/nutri-score-update-changes-2024)
- A study of about 46,752 French products found the updated algorithm agrees better with French food-based dietary guidelines. — [Santé publique France study](https://www.santepubliquefrance.fr/index.php/nutrition-et-activite-physique/article/consistency-initial-and-updated-version-nutri-score-food-based-dietary-guidelines-a-french)

**NOVA / ultra-processing**
- Hall et al. 2019 (Cell Metab 30:67–77): 20 inpatients ate ultra-processed or unprocessed diets ad libitum for 14 days each. The two diets were matched for presented calories, sugar, fat, sodium, fibre and macronutrients. On the ultra-processed diet people ate about 500 kcal/day more and gained 0.9 kg; on the unprocessed diet they lost 0.9 kg. — [NIH news release](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/nih-study-finds-heavily-processed-foods-cause-overeating-weight-gain); [paper PDF](https://www.foodpolitics.com/wp-content/uploads/Kevin-Hall_Ultraprocessed_Cell-Metab_19.pdf)
- Limitation: the study was not designed to separate out the effects of specific macronutrients. — [Lustig commentary](https://robertlustig.com/2019/07/2215/)
- How to define UPF is still contested ("it depends who you ask"), and industry pushes back. — [FoodNavigator, Jun 2025](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/06/26/ultra-processed-food-definitions-explained/)

**ZOE (0–100 food score) and Lifesum (food rating, Life Score)**
- ZOE's score combines blood-sugar impact, blood-fat impact, gut health, fat quality, UPF status, salt, GI, sugar and fibre. Examples: white bread 0, sweet potato 40, avocado 95, kale 97. ZOE also has a separate 5-level processing-risk scale. The UK Food & Drink Federation says it "contradicts government's healthy eating advice". ZOE sells supplements and gut-shot drinks. — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/new-app-from-zoe-labels-food-risk-in-seconds/); [VegNews](https://vegnews.com/zoe-app-ultra-processed-food-risk); [Country & Town House](https://www.countryandtownhouse.com/?p=380014)
- Lifesum rates each food per 100 kcal, not per serving, using macro/micronutrient balance, calorie density and food type. Ratings change with the chosen diet (for example keto lowers high-carb foods). — [Lifesum help: food rating](https://help.lifesum.com/en/article/food-rating-1afp5ga/)
- Lifesum's Life Score is a weekly score from 0 to 150 built from 16 nutrition and exercise measures, with feedback on what went well and what to improve. — [Lifesum help: Life Score](https://help.lifesum.com/en/article/life-score-android-afv0fw/)

**Evidence on front-of-pack and traffic-light labels**
- A network meta-analysis of 134 studies (QMUL 2021) found that traffic lights, Nutri-Score, Chilean warnings and health warnings all steered purchases toward healthier products. — [QMUL news](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/news/latest-news/2021/smd/new-study-supports-call-for-mandatory-front-of-pack-labelling-to-improve-diets.html)
- A review of 5 RCTs on traffic-light labels found no positive effect in 3 of them. It concludes that traffic-light labels alone may have no substantial effect. — [JNFS systematic review](https://publish.kne-publishing.com/index.php/JNFS/article/view/10206)
- Temple's 2020 review found few real-supermarket studies and only a small effect (under 2.0%). Braesco & Drewnowski's 2023 review of 65 studies calls effectiveness "still debated". — [Braesco & Drewnowski 2023, PMC9824714](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9824714/); [Temple 2020 (Athabasca)](https://pure.athabascau.ca/en/publications/front-of-package-food-labels-a-narrative-review/)

**Moralising foods**
- An implicit-association study found that people link calorie density with moral attributes ("sinful" foods) in healthy, anorexic and orthorexic groups alike. — [PMC9234570](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9234570/)
- A Flinders University study, reported in Sept 2026, found that 1 in 5 of 300 TikTok/Instagram food posts framed food as "good" or "bad", and linked this to restriction and guilt. — [1News, 27 Sep 2026](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/09/27/eat-like-me-to-look-like-me-the-slippery-slope-into-orthorexia/) (news coverage only; the paper was not read)
- The Alliance for Eating Disorders advises against moral food labels because they add shame and increase preoccupation. — [Alliance for Eating Disorders](https://www.allianceforeatingdisorders.com/removing-food-morality/)

### Inferences
- Energy density (Noom's basis) and Nutri-Score are the most defensible open signals. Nutri-Score is public, documented and validated, and the 2024 version fixes the sweetener loophole. Open Food Facts already publishes Nutri-Score and NOVA, so the app could show them as product facts rather than verdicts on the user.
- Yuka and ZOE show the risk of proprietary composite scores: regulators and industry criticise them as opaque, and including additives or organic status adds weightings that are hard to defend.
- Label effects in the real world are small. A coach should not expect a badge or colour alone to change behaviour; an explanation plus a concrete next action is likely needed.
- A colour scale that ends in red or orange will be read as moral. If colours are used at all, use neutral words ("lighter / denser") and keep everything off a red scale. This fits the app's ban on red guilt states.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed RCT of Noom's colour system on its own was found.
- Fooducate's current formula is not documented after 2014.
- No independent validation was found of Yuka's or ZOE's scores against health outcomes.
- Lose It! and MyFitnessPal food-quality signals were not researched (search returned nothing).

## 2. Swap suggestions

### Takeaway
Swaps are a core feature (Yuka "alternatives", Fooducate "better choices", the Noom colour shift). Trial evidence shows people do use swap prompts, at least for sugar and saturated fat, but measured changes in nutrients are small, and only feasibility-level data exist.

### Cited Findings
- SwapSHOP feasibility RCT (Piernas et al., JMIR mHealth 2024): a UK grocery app with healthier-swap suggestions, goal setting and feedback, compared with logging only. 81% (saturated fat arm) and 87% (sugar arm) of participants used swaps. The salt arm was too small to analyse. People were willing to swap for sugar and saturated fat but not for salt, and the exploratory nutrient reductions were small. — [JMIR mHealth 2024 e45854](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2024/1/e45854/PDF); [ISRCTN13022312](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN13022312)
- A randomised online-grocery trial of 1,201 US adults (reported 2026) found that health, climate and combined swap prompts each led to healthier selections than control. About 25% of offers were accepted. — [ReachMD](https://reachmd.com/news/online-grocery-swap-prompts-improve-diet-quality-cut-carbon/2488217/) (secondary summary)
- Yuka's swaps have been criticised for ignoring convenience and context. — [Mama Knows Nutrition](https://mamaknowsnutrition.com/yuka-app-reviews)
- Fooducate ranks products within their category, so a swap stays in the same category (cereal for cereal). — [Health Advocate](https://blog.healthadvocate.com/2014/03/wellness-app-review-fooducate/)

### Inferences
- Swaps should stay within the same category ("another breakfast cereal with more fibre"), be optional, and work as "try this next time", not "you chose wrong".
- About 25% acceptance is realistic, so the app should not push swaps on every log. One well-chosen suggestion per day or meal probably beats many.
- Salt swaps were the least accepted. Additive ideas (add vegetables, add beans) may suit salt better than substitution.

### Gaps
- No evaluations were found for Change4Life Sugar Swaps or SaltSwitch.
- No published data were found on how many Yuka users take its alternatives.

## 3. Daily and weekly feedback

### Takeaway
Weekly summaries are common (Lifesum's Life Score), but they usually include a score of the user, which this app bans. The evidence supports feedback on behaviour (what was eaten, what to try next) rather than on the person.

### Cited Findings
- Lifesum's weekly Life Score (0–150 from 16 metrics) gives feedback on what went well and what to improve. — [Lifesum help](https://help.lifesum.com/en/article/life-score-android-afv0fw/)
- Samdal et al. 2017 found that, over the long term, "feedback on outcomes of behaviour" was one of three BCTs explaining all between-study variation. — [Samdal 2017, PMC5370453](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5370453)
- A retrospective study of Noom users (N=11,252, self-reported weight) linked weight loss to engagement: articles read, meals logged, messages to the coach, weigh-ins. — [JMIR mHealth 2021 e30622, PMC8663454](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8663454) (association only, not causation)

### Inferences
- A weekly "what went well / one thing to try" summary keeps the useful feedback BCT without a score of the user. Nutrient-highlight phrasing ("fibre was around 18 g on most days; beans or wholegrain bread at lunch would add about 5 g") turns guideline gaps into actions.

### Gaps
- No studies were found comparing daily and weekly feedback timing in diet apps.
- No details were found on the MyFitnessPal, Lose It! or MFP AI assistant insight formats (searches returned nothing).

## 4. Behaviour-change techniques (BCTs) that drive outcomes

### Takeaway
Goal setting (for behaviour), self-monitoring of behaviour, feedback, and an autonomy-supportive (motivational-interviewing) style have the best meta-regression support. Most apps use few BCTs, and often not the most effective ones.

### Cited Findings
- Samdal et al. 2017 (IJBNPA 14:42): 48 RCTs in adults with overweight or obesity. Effect size was 0.37 short term and 0.24 long term. Goal setting and self-monitoring of behaviour predicted effects at both time points. Long term, MI-style autonomy support, goal setting of behaviour and feedback explained all between-study variation. The authors say a "person-centred, autonomy-supportive" approach seems important for maintenance. — [PMC5370453](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5370453)
- A review of 40 diet and activity apps found 8.1 BCTs per app on average (range 2–18). The most common were "provide instruction" (83%), "set graded tasks" (70%) and "prompt self-monitoring" (60%). Effective BCTs were more common in paid apps. — [BMC Public Health 2014, PMC4080693](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4080693)
- Children's diet apps averaged 6 BCTs, and the most common ones were not the most effective. — [Frontiers Public Health 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2022.803152/pdf)
- Noom: in a Korean metabolic-syndrome study (Toro-Ramos et al.), participants lost 7.5% of body weight at 15 weeks and kept off about 5.2% at 52 weeks, versus matched controls. The figures come from a company-promoted press release. — [Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/view/content/study-in-the-journal-of-metabolic-syndrome-and-related-disorders-20040762)
- Noom launched the Welli AI chatbot in June 2024 to support human coaching (24/7 answers, GLP-1 support, high-protein recipes). The CEO said the efficiency gains moved human coaches into a premium tier. — [Noom press release via FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/press-releases/noom-introduces-ai-enabled-products-to-enhance-on-demand-health-care-and-interactive-coaching); [TechTarget](https://techtarget.com/searchenterpriseai/news/366598870/Noom-CEO-on-the-need-for-AI-in-wellness-and-health)

### Inferences
- The app's existing logging covers self-monitoring. Missing BCTs to add without dark patterns: user-chosen behaviour goals (not outcome goals such as weight), gentle feedback on behaviour, implementation-intention prompts ("when I make lunch, I'll add a handful of beans"), and MI-style questions in the AI chat ("what would feel doable this week?").
- Feedback should be about behaviour, not about the person.

### Gaps
- No primary sources were fetched on Noom's CBT curriculum structure, or on implementation-intention and habit-stacking trials specifically in diet apps.

## 5. Non-counting frameworks: energy density (Volumetrics) and plate models

### Takeaway
Energy density and plate models give calorie-light coaching rules ("fill half the plate with vegetables", "pick water-rich foods") that need no counting and fit a no-guilt app.

### Cited Findings
- Rolls' work: in a Penn State study, lowering energy density reduced intake about twice as much as cutting portion size, and combining both worked best. Satiety studies used water-adjusted preloads to isolate the effect of density. — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/blog/food-thought/fooling-satiety-meter-recipe); [PMC4182946](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4182946)
- Red Pen Reviews says the core claim that low-density diets reduce intake and weight is supported, but the long-term effect on weight is probably not large. — [Red Pen Reviews](https://www.redpenreviews.org/?p=1652)
- Harvard Healthy Eating Plate: half vegetables and fruit (more vegetables than fruit; potatoes don't count), a quarter whole grains, a quarter healthy protein (fish, poultry, beans, nuts; limit red meat, avoid processed meat), healthy oils, and water/coffee/tea instead of sugary drinks. It was designed to fix gaps in USDA MyPlate. — [Baking Business](https://www.bakingbusiness.com/articles/41317-harvard-researchers-unveil-healthy-eating-plate); [Harvard Dining](https://dining.harvard.edu/campus-dining/undergraduate-dining/dining-harvard/nutrition-dietary-preferences)
- French PNNS 4 (2019, Santé publique France): at least 5 fruit and vegetables a day (fresh, frozen or canned); pulses at least twice a week (now grouped with protein foods); a small handful of nuts a day; at least one wholegrain starchy food a day. In the 2021 Baromètre, only 19% of men and 25% of women met the fruit and vegetable target. — [Santé publique France recommendations PDF (Jan 2019)](https://chambery.ufcquechoisir.fr/wp-content/uploads/sites/16/2019/03/recommandations-alimentation-janvier-2019.pdf); [Santé publique France](https://www.santepubliquefrance.fr/content/download/720344/4663447?version=1)

### Inferences
- These frameworks map directly onto "add" coaching on a logged meal: "half the plate as vegetables", "swap to the wholegrain version", "pulses twice this week". Energy density can be computed from the per-100 g kcal the app is adding, and shown neutrally ("light/medium/dense").

### Gaps
- No modern meta-analysis of energy-density interventions was found. The primary Harvard nutritionsource page and the Manger Bouger site were not fetched.

## 6. Evidence-based guidelines usable as coach rules

### Takeaway
A small set of WHO and EFSA thresholds can drive daily and weekly coach rules: free sugars <10% of energy (ideally <5%), salt <5 g/day, fibre ≥25 g/day, fruit and vegetables ≥400 g/day.

### Cited Findings
- WHO: free sugars <10% of energy, with a further reduction to <5% for extra benefit. In WHO Europe's translation that is about 50 g (12 tsp), or 25 g at 5%. Free sugars include those in honey, syrups and fruit juice. — [WHO healthy diet fact sheet](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/healthy-diet-factsheet394); [WHO Europe](https://who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/nutrition---maintaining-a-healthy-lifestyle)
- WHO: salt <5 g/day (<2 g sodium). — [WHO Europe](https://who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/nutrition---maintaining-a-healthy-lifestyle)
- WHO: at least 400 g of fruit and vegetables a day for adults and children over 10, and 250–350 g for younger children. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/westernpacific/health-topics/healthy-diet/10)
- EFSA 2010: fibre 25 g/day is adequate for bowel function in adults, and higher intakes bring more benefit; children 2 g/MJ. — [EFSA press release](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/es/press/news/nda100326)
- EU Reference Intakes for labelling (Reg. 1169/2011 Annex XIII): energy 8400 kJ / 2000 kcal, fat 70 g, saturates 20 g, carbohydrate 260 g, sugars 90 g, protein 50 g, salt 6 g. — [EUR-Lex Reg 1169/2011](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2011/1169/oj) (values from prior knowledge; not re-fetched this session, so verify)

### Inferences
- Coach rules should be stated as weekly patterns and ranges, not daily pass/fail. Note that the EU "sugars 90 g" reference is total sugars, not WHO free sugars, so the two should not be mixed. The app cannot tell free sugars from intrinsic sugars using per-100 g label data alone, so sugar messages should be soft and framed around drinks and sweets.

### Gaps
- No explicit WHO numeric limit on ultra-processed foods was found. National UPF guidance (for example Brazil and France PNNS "limit") was not verified in this session.

## 7. Criticism and harms

### Takeaway
Calorie and fitness tracking is associated with eating-disorder symptoms in vulnerable users. Noom paid a US$62M settlement over auto-renewal dark patterns. WW's model is commercially under strain. All of this supports the app's no-guilt, no-score design.

### Cited Findings
- Simpson & Mazzeo 2017 (493 undergraduates): calorie tracking was associated with food-related anxiety and restrictive dieting. Fitness tracking was the stronger independent predictor of ED symptoms. — [Psychology in Action](https://www.psychologyinaction.org/2018-4-29-calorie-counting-and-fitness-tracking-technology-associations-with-eating-disorder-symptomology/); [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/za/blog/the-truth-about-exercise-addiction/201702/do-fitness-trackers-promote-eating-disorders)
- Levinson et al. 2017 (about 105 people with diagnosed eating disorders): 73% of MyFitnessPal users felt the app contributed to their ED, and this perception correlated with symptoms. — [PMC8485346 (secondary review)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8485346); [Duke Psychiatry blog](https://psychiatry.duke.edu/blog/trouble-tracking)
- Noom: in Feb 2022, Mahood v. Noom (S.D.N.Y. 1:20-cv-03677) settled for $62M ($56M cash plus $6M credits) over "risk-free" trials that auto-renewed and were hard to cancel. A former engineer testified that cancellation was deliberately made difficult. Noom committed to 100% self-service cancellation. — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2022/02/14/noom-settlement); [Deceptive Design case](https://www.deceptive.design/cases/geraldine-mahood-v-noom-inc); [Globe and Mail](https://theglobeandmail.com/business/international-business/article-noom-diet-app-reaches-us62-million-settlement-over-automatic)
- WW filed for Chapter 11 in May 2025. — [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/health/article/weightwatchers-files-for-bankruptcy/)
- Dietitians warn that number-heavy apps can worsen people's relationship with food. ZOE replies that its scores encourage positive behaviour. — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/new-app-from-zoe-labels-food-risk-in-seconds/)

### Inferences
- Showing grams and nutrients needs safeguards: an opt-out or "calm mode", no red states, no daily deficit framing, and wellbeing or ED signposting.

### Gaps
- No Linardon diet-app meta-analysis was located in this session (search failed). No FTC action against Noom was found; only the private class action was. The final settlement approval was not confirmed.

## 8. Tone and phrasing

### Takeaway
For prevention behaviours such as healthy eating, gain-framed and "add" framing has a small, context-dependent edge. Autonomy support matters more than the frame, and reactance is a real risk.

### Cited Findings
- A meta-analysis of 94 studies found gain frames slightly more effective for prevention behaviours (r ≈ 0.083, small). — [PMC4117640](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4117640)
- Reviews conclude gain frames suit prevention (including healthy eating) and loss frames suit detection (screening). — [Frontiers Psychology 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.583209/full)
- Gain frames worked better when autonomy was primed, and only for the most relevant group (BMI over 25). Both frames can trigger psychological reactance. — [PMC8430703](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8430703); [Luiss 2025 PDF](https://iris.luiss.it/retrieve/019961de-1be5-473b-9136-9e03a3642d27/1-s2.0-S0969698925003625-main.pdf)
- Positive "eat broccoli and live longer" messages beat negative ones for fruit and vegetables (secondary blog). — [OUP blog 2015](https://blog.oup.com/2015/01/which-health-messages-work-positive-negative/)
- Samdal 2017: an autonomy-supportive, MI-style approach predicts long-term maintenance. — [PMC5370453](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5370453)

### Inferences
- Suggested phrasing patterns: "add" before "avoid" ("add a fistful of beans" rather than "cut carbs"); options, not orders ("one idea, if you like"); describe the food, not the person; give one suggestion at a time.

### Gaps
- No RCT was found that tests "add" versus "avoid" phrasing inside a diet app.
